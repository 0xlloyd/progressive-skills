# AI defaults

Use this catalogue to label options. An option is an **AI default** when it matches a choice AI tools commonly make with no project-specific reason; it is **bespoke** when it traces to the brief, a named inspiration or a research finding.
These are labels, not bans: a default is fine when the user picks it knowingly, for example a brand that already owns purple.
Defaults shift as tools and training data change, so treat this list as a starting point and add new patterns as you notice them.

## Two waves

- **First wave** is what AI tools produce with no direction: the "statistical average" of modern web UI.
- **Second wave** is what they produce when told to look "less AI" or "more premium". These are just as generic now and should be labelled the same way.

## Colour

- **First wave:** indigo or violet primaries (Tailwind indigo-500 `#6366F1`, violet-500 `#8B5CF6`, purple-500 `#A855F7`); purple-to-blue or purple-to-pink gradients, especially on headline text and buttons; cool slate or zinc greys; white or very light grey backgrounds; cyan or neon accents on dark backgrounds; glowing gradient blobs.
- **Second wave:** warm cream or off-white background with a terracotta, rust or burnt-orange accent; near-black background with one acid green, lime or electric accent; muted sage green with beige; "editorial" black and white with one red.
- **Structural tells:** gradients with no meaning, a palette with no named roles, success/warning/error colours taken straight from a framework.

## Typography

- **First wave:** Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, Helvetica or the system font stack everywhere; one family at one or two weights; tight negative letter-spacing on every headline.
- **Second wave:** Space Grotesk, Geist, DM Sans, Manrope, Plus Jakarta Sans, Satoshi, General Sans; Instrument Serif, Playfair Display or Fraunces for "editorial" headlines paired with a geometric sans; monospace eyebrow labels.
- **Structural tells:** no reason given for the pairing; type scale picked from a preset ratio with no testing in real copy.

## Layout

- Centred hero with a headline, subline and one or two buttons.
- Row of three feature cards, each with an icon, a title and two lines.
- Bento grids of unequal rounded tiles.
- Small uppercase "eyebrow" label above every section heading.
- Logo strip of "trusted by" customers under the hero.
- Every section the same width, padding and rhythm.

## Shape and elevation

- Large uniform corner radius on everything (around 12–24px), often cards inside cards.
- Soft, low-opacity drop shadows on every surface.
- Glassmorphism (frosted translucent panels) and thin 1px light borders on dark cards.

## Motion

- Fade-and-rise on scroll for every element.
- Bounce or scale-up on every hover.
- One duration and easing for everything (around 300ms ease-in-out).
- Social: static carousels only, or slides that just slide in; typewriter text; confetti and sparkle effects.
- Animated gradient blobs and glowing borders that move with no meaning.

## Web components

- Sticky white header with logo left, links centred, one filled button right.
- Pill-shaped primary buttons in the accent colour with a soft shadow.
- Testimonial carousel of three cards with circular avatars and five stars.
- Pricing table with three tiers and the middle one raised.
- Four-column footer of link lists with social icons and a newsletter field.
- Toasts in the bottom-right corner, skeleton loaders in light grey.

## Social and carousel templates

- Cover with a big emoji or 3D icon, centred title and "swipe" arrow.
- Every slide the same layout with a number in a circle.
- Chat-screenshot slides with no framing.
- End slide saying "Follow for more" with a repost icon.

## Imagery and icons

- Abstract 3D blobs, mesh gradients, or glossy 3D objects.
- Stock line icon sets used unmodified (for example Lucide or Heroicons) and emoji used as icons.
- Generic AI-generated photography with soft lighting and no real place.

## Voice and copy

- Words like streamline, empower, supercharge, seamless, unlock, elevate, leverage, world-class, next-generation.
- Aphoristic taglines ("Build faster. Ship smarter.") and three-part slogans.
- Headlines that could describe any product in the category.

## How to label

1. Compare the option with this catalogue and with what a fresh AI tool would produce for the same brief. Once the user has made choices, the AI default for the next decision is the most predictable option within that direction (for example, plain black for an impact slide in a pastel palette), not the generic default from this list, because every option shown must stay cohesive with what's already decided.
2. If it matches and has no project-specific source, label it **AI default**.
3. If it matches but also has a genuine source (the brand already owns that colour, the inspiration uses that face), label it **bespoke** and name the source, noting that it resembles a common default.
4. Never present a default as bespoke to make an option look better.
